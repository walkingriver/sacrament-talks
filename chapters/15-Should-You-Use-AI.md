# Should You Use AI to Help Write Your Talk?

In recent years, artificial intelligence tools have become increasingly accessible and powerful. With the rise of ChatGPT and other large language models, we've seen both excitement and concern about AI's capabilities. Schools and universities have grappled with students using these tools to write essays and complete assignments, leading many institutions to implement strict policies about AI use. But when it comes to preparing a sacrament meeting talk, you might wonder: Is it appropriate to use AI as a tool? Let's explore this question together.

First, let's acknowledge something important: The most crucial aspects of preparing a sacrament talk are prayer, personal revelation, and your own spiritual preparation. No AI tool can replace these essential elements. However, just as we might use a computer to type our talk or a scripture app to look up references, AI can be a helpful assistant when used thoughtfully and appropriately.

## When AI Can Be Helpful

Think of AI as a brainstorming partner or a writing assistant. When you're stuck getting started, it can help you organize your thoughts or suggest different ways to approach your topic. For example, if you're speaking about faith, you might ask an AI tool to help you outline the key principles you want to cover.

AI can also be helpful for:

- Suggesting ways to make your writing clearer and more engaging
- Finding relevant scripture references (which you should always verify)
- Helping you manage your time more effectively
- Checking your grammar and readability

In fact, the Church itself has embraced AI technology in a careful, measured way. The General Handbook now includes an AI-powered search assistant that helps leaders and members find answers to their questions. This feature, available in the Gospel Library app and on ChurchofJesusChrist.org, can analyze questions and summarize relevant information from the handbook. However, even in this official Church tool, users are instructed to verify all responses using the provided handbook references - a good reminder that AI should be used as a helper, not the final authority.

## When Not to Use AI

Remember that your sacrament talk should be a personal message, not a computer-generated presentation. Avoid using AI to:

- Write the entire talk for you
- Generate personal experiences or testimonies
- Make doctrinal statements without verification
- Replace your own study and preparation

## Using AI Wisely

If you choose to use AI tools, here are some guidelines to follow:

1. Always start with prayer and personal study
2. Use AI as a supplement to, not a replacement for, your own preparation
3. Carefully review and verify any scripture references or quotes
4. Maintain your personal voice and experiences
5. Remember that the Spirit, not artificial intelligence, is your most important guide

Consider this approach: You might use AI to help you create an initial outline or to suggest scripture references, but then rely on your own study, prayer, and personal experiences to develop the actual content. This way, you're using technology as a tool while keeping the sacred nature of your assignment at the forefront.

That third guideline about verifying references is particularly important, and I learned this lesson the hard way. Let me share a cautionary tale from my own experience.

## A Real Life Cautionary Tale

While researching this book, I used ChatGPT to help me find some relevant quotes and stories from General Conference. I was specifically looking for a humorous story by Pres. Hinckley. He was not afraid to use humor, so I thought he'd be great to use in my of example using humor in your talks. I gave the following prompt to ChatGPT:

> Pres. Hinckley was especially good at injecting just the right amount of humor into his conference talks. He managed to keep it both relevant and reverent. Can you find an example and provide a link?

It gave me this response, exactly...

> President Gordon B. Hinckley was indeed skilled at incorporating humor into his conference talks while maintaining a reverent and spiritually uplifting atmosphere. One example of this can be found in his October 1994 General Conference talk, “God Is at the Helm.”
>
> In this talk, President Hinckley humorously addresses his age by saying, “I am an old man. I do not know how much longer I have to live. But whatever the time, I shall continue to cry repentance and try to help others.”
>
> Though he is lighthearted in mentioning his age, President Hinckley seamlessly transitions back to the primary message of his talk, emphasizing the importance of repentance and seeking to help others in their spiritual journey.
>
> You can find the full transcript of this talk here: https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/1994/10/god-is-at-the-helm?lang=eng

The quote was reasonably amusing and sounded exactly like the Pres. Hinckley I remember. ChatGPT gave me the title, date, and conference session of the talk. It even provided a URL to the talk itself. In short, it was pretty much what I asked for.

The trouble is, it was completely made up. Pres. Hinckley never said that, at least not in that talk. I couldn't find that quote anywhere on the church website. In fact, the link I showed above goes nowhere. He did give a talk with that title, but six months before.

Why did this happen? AI tools like ChatGPT don't actually understand or know facts the way humans do. Instead, they work by analyzing patterns in the vast amount of text they've been trained on and generating responses that are statistically likely to follow those patterns. Think of it like someone who has read thousands of conference talks and can mimic the style perfectly, but doesn't actually remember which prophet said what. When asked a specific question, these AI tools will try to create a response that "sounds right" based on everything they've seen – even if that means combining different pieces of information in ways that aren't actually true. This is what we call "hallucination" in AI terms.

Other quotes that I have used in this book were also found by ChatGPT, and they were spot on. I'd say about 25% of my initial research ended up being hallucinations. Can you imagine if I'd gone through with publishing the book with those glaring errors? I'm sure there are plenty of those as it is, but these types of errors would be a line I wouldn't want to cross.

So, go ahead and use them to help you with your research, but always verify the information you get from AI tools.

## A Final Thought

The key question isn't whether using AI is right or wrong, but rather how we can use it wisely and appropriately while maintaining the sacred nature of our calling to speak in sacrament meeting. When used thoughtfully, AI can be one of many tools that help us prepare more effectively, allowing us to focus our energy on the most important aspects of our preparation: prayer, personal revelation, and sharing our authentic testimony.
